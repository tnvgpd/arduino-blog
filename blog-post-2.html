My First Project: Sensor-Based Nightlight

Published on May 24, 2026

To test out some fundamental Arduino skills, I completed my first project: a sensor-based nightlight. Using the Grove Light Sensor, I created a system that reacts to light deficiency by automatically turning on an LED.

I connected the light sensor to an analog pin and created an integer variable to store the sensor’s readings. Since I initially didn’t know the ideal sensor threshold, I experimented with different values by gradually increasing and decreasing them based on the results. After plenty of testing, I found that 900 worked as an effective threshold.

Through experimentation, I discovered that lower light conditions produced higher sensor values, while brighter environments resulted in lower values. Once I understood this pattern, I wrote a simple if-else statement that turned the LED on whenever the sensor value exceeded 900 and turned it off when the value was less than or equal to 900.

To better observe the results, I also used Serial.print() to display the sensor readings in the serial monitor.

This was a great first experiment, and I’m excited to continue learning through more Arduino projects.
